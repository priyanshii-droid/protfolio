Chapter II • Flagship Project

An Idea Called Saarthi

The story of reimagining digital banking from the lens of human hesitation, missing context, and quiet empathy.

Scene 2.1 • The Spark

“What if digital banking understood what people actually needed?”

I

I walked into the hackathon workspace with a notebook tucked under my arm, watching teams scramble to add more charts, faster crypto tickers, and flashier buttons.

Yet, whenever I watched people in real life—like my parents or first-time digital users—open their banking apps, they didn't want flashiness. They hesitated. They double-checked account numbers with furrowed brows. They were paralyzed by cold technical jargon.

The Vision

Meet Saarthi.

A smarter approach to making digital banking more understandable, accessible, and context-aware—turning anxiety into quiet confidence.

Scene 2.2 • The Research

“Digital banking offers countless services, but understanding what to do, finding the right information and managing financial tasks can still be confusing.”

T

To design Saarthi, I began by gathering the actual complaints and moments of anxiety people experience with their banking portals.

The problem was rarely technical downtime. It was human uncertainty:

  • The Context Void: Spreadsheets of transaction rows that record amounts, but fail to explain why spending fluctuated or what safe disposable income remains.
  • The Fear of Irreversibility: Users afraid to make simple transfers because banking warnings sound punitive rather than helpful.
  • The Disconnected Ecosystem: Multiple separate tabs for checking statements, investigating disputes, testing budgets, and contacting support.

We didn't need another generic banking dashboard. We needed a system that acts as a co-pilot.

Scene 2.3 • The Blueprint

From pencil strokes in a notebook to the foundational Saarthi architecture.

O

In my sketchbook, I began drawing what a truly calm financial workspace would look like. No cluttered ads. No screaming red banners.

Instead, three clear principles guided the sketch:

1
Evidence First: Ground every piece of advice in an immutable, canonical ledger. The AI should never invent numbers.
2
Safe Simulation: Give users a "Money Time Machine" so they can simulate choices before committing real funds.
3
Natural Interaction: Allow people to ask in their own words or speak via voice rather than navigating complex menus.

Scene 2.4 • The Machinery

Inside Saarthi: Conceptual Architecture & Implemented Reality

To build an assistant people can trust with their life savings, the system cannot be an unconstrained chatbot. It requires deterministic mathematical precision paired with intelligent contextual orchestration.

🧠

AI Orchestrator

Implemented in Prototype

Receives queries and runs deterministic checks first (identifying merchant categories, calculating variance) before allowing the AI model to explain findings in natural language.

Multi-tool routing Safe explanations
⚖️

Financial Engine

Implemented in Prototype

Normalizes raw imports (Excel, CSV, JSON) into a strict Canonical Ledger. Enforces mathematical precision for income, expense totals, and savings rate calculations.

Canonical Ledger Zero Hallucination
🗂️

User Memory

Concept / Planned

Designed to retain long-term contextual awareness across sessions (recurring bills, seasonal patterns, personalized budget thresholds) without storing unencrypted credentials.

Pattern tracking Privacy-first
🌐

Web Intelligence

Concept / Planned

Enables Saarthi to retrieve real-time external financial context: benchmark interest rates, verified merchant policies, and regulatory RBI guidelines for dispute resolution.

Live benchmarks Fact verification

Scene 2.6 • The Horizon

“Building Saarthi taught me that creating technology is not just about making something work. It is about understanding the problem, questioning assumptions and turning an idea into something people can actually use.”

A

Before Saarthi, I thought engineering was about writing clever algorithms and finishing project milestones.

Building Saarthi forced me to step outside of my code editor. It meant listening to people describe their financial stress, watching someone hesitate before tapping a screen, and realizing that clarity is a moral choice in interface design.

“One project opened a thousand new questions.”

Questions about how we structure data, how we preserve accessibility, and how a student continues to grow. That curiosity carries directly into my ongoing learning journey.

Chapter III • The Craft

Still Figuring Things Out

The everyday reality of a Computer Science student: debugging, exploring, failing, and finding joy in the process.

T

If there is one truth I have embraced in my journey as a student, it is that expertise is not a destination where you suddenly have all the answers. It is simply becoming comfortable with the messy, humbling act of figuring things out.

Some days are spent wrestling with memory allocations, recursion trees, and edge cases in C++ and Data Structures. Other days are immersed in CSS transforms, SVG animations, and understanding why a particular layout feels intuitive to a human eye.

I don’t pretend to have mastered it all. I am reading papers, building small prototypes, breaking things in my terminal, asking questions that feel naive, and learning how to think clearly. That is the philosophy behind “Still Becoming”.
